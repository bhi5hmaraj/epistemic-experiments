## What this retreat is about

EE Alignment Retreat 3.0 is four days of sessions, co-created by the people who attend.

A constitution alone cannot align a government with its citizens. The culture has to do part of
the work, and every citizen has a role in it.

We think the same holds for AI. To address misaligned AI, we want to start by aligning
ourselves. Then we extend that alignment
[outwards](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/towards-a-scale-free-theory-of-intelligent): to our
community, our environment and, in time, our technology.

The sessions range from walks in the nearby forest, yoga, meditation and circling to making
sense of the state of the world. In some workshops we check which parts of our work we want to
automate and which parts we want to do ourselves. When we rush to solve a problem, we miss how
it connects to other things, and our solution creates new problems.

This is a bottom-up event in the style of an unconference. If your interest lies outside these
topics, propose your own session.

## How it started

The first retreat was in June 2024. One of us offered a house in Ooty to a group of friends who
wanted to make sense of AI. It grew from a side project called Horcruxes for Humanity, which
looked for ways to stay resilient to black swan events. We wrote poems, debated, made
predictions and walked around Ooty.

Ooty 2.0, in June 2025, kept those roots. It had prediction markets, a workshop on Bayesian
thinking and a lot of vibe coding. Between them we put grounding sessions: yoga, meditation,
circling, and talks on Buddhism and the metacrisis. We also discussed how to help our families
and friends through the coming intelligence revolution.

Some of the vibe coding became projects that still run, such as
[Simulacra](https://simulacra.cc) and
[Live Conversational Threads](https://github.com/anantham/live_conversational_threads). Read our
[reflections from Ooty 2.0](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/KerjdwMehqrHDHEhJ/reflections-from-ooty-retreat-2-0)
on LessWrong.

Now we want to keep the tradition alive and open it to a wider group. Join us for the third
iteration.

## What we noticed

- People felt free to drop their masks. Conversations, arguments and debates were honest, and it
  was a relief to stop playing status games.
- Slowing down helped. With time and the sustained attention of a group, we went deep into hard
  questions and did not feel overwhelmed.
- Things kept going afterwards: friendships, collaborations, and projects that started as
  weekend experiments.

We hope to grow a community in India that practises alignment in this broader sense, with room
for rich friendships and good collaborations.

We value decentralised, local work on the ground. Money, talent and attention in AI safety flow
through a few pipelines that are linked to each other. Even when everyone involved is sincere,
that makes it easy for everyone to defer to the same views. We want spaces where people make
sense of AI on their own terms. That includes approaches to alignment that differ from the
dominant ones in rationalist and EA circles.

## Themes

Participants host the sessions, so the structure grows from what they bring. These are the
themes we hope to see. Under each theme are sessions that people have proposed so far. They are
early drafts, and the schedule is still open.

### Embodiment and integrity

Talk about alignment tends to stay intellectual and conceptual. We want time away from
conversations, writing and code: in nature, walking, moving, staring at the sky. Then the whole
body takes part in our decisions.

Expect meditation, yoga, treks and optional circling. The key question: how far do you live by
the beliefs you hold, and if you struggle to, why?

- **Hammer time.** Peer-to-peer problem solving and star circling.

### Cognitive gym

AI makes it easy to skip the struggle, and
[the struggle is where the learning happens](https://www.justinmath.com/why-is-the-edtech-industry-so-damn-soft/).
How do we [keep our minds fit](https://justinmath.com/how-to-do-cognitive-weightlifting/) when
AI can do so much of our thinking? We would also love interactive explainers. If you understand
something well, build something that helps the rest of us understand it. Think of
[Bret Victor](https://worrydream.com), [3Blue1Brown](https://www.3blue1brown.com) and
[Nicky Case](https://ncase.me).

- **Co-writing session.** Timed writing without AI, with feedback from each other, on realistic
  fiction about the next 30 years. Writing and thinking are the same.
- **Hands-on puzzles.** No AI, no phones. A robot arm, Turing Tumble, Spintronics, maths
  puzzles, and a game coded by hand on paper.

### Sensemaking and forecasting

What is happening in the world, where is it heading, and what follows from what you believe? We
like falsifiable predictions. We change our minds when reality shows us where we were wrong.

- **State of the world mapping.** Politics, climate, AI, wars, the economy and more, mapped
  together on a big canvas.
- **Debate (Stoa).** One big question, everyone's position and confidence, then red-teaming,
  cruxes and predictions. Candidate questions: Is the fertility crisis a big deal? Are technical
  approaches to AI safety more important than the others? Should we grant AIs personhood?
- **Five-year plans.** Plans for several AI futures at once. What does each future mean for
  education, meaning, money and relationships, and for India in particular?

### Metacrisis

Is there a shared operating system behind the major crises humanity faces? Does the way we
innovate and solve problems create new problems? What would it mean to work at the level of
causes?

- **Crisis generator functions.** Looking for the patterns behind the crises, and for ways to act
  that help more than they harm.

### Education

What does it mean to be educated in a world transformed by AI? What should young people learn,
and what should schools and universities become?

- **Goals of education.** Builds on an online discussion group from earlier this year, on
  education in a post-AGI world.

### Technology in daily life

When doing becomes easy, how and why we do it matters more. How do we keep our tools working for
what we want?

- **[Interface integration therapy](http://groundless.ai/interface).** 30 minutes of one-to-one
  help to automate the parts of your work you dread, or to change your habits around devices.

### Community and cooperation

AI makes building cheaper, and a small grant goes further than it did a few years ago. What
should a community like ours fund and build together, and how would we know it helped? We plan
to launch our own request for proposals to support independent researchers and projects in
India.

We believe communities and relationships will matter more as traditional institutions come under
strain. AI empowers the individual more than the collective. We want to explore how to mitigate
this, or be proven wrong.

- **Tools for community cooperation and resilience.** What to fund, how to measure impact with
  honesty, and whether a small Manifund-style granting group makes sense for us.

## Bring your own session

Everyone comes with something to give: a rough thought, a half-baked idea, an unreleased
project. You choose the prompts and the format. A session can be a discussion or a debate. You
can also ask the group to help you or to teach you.

A session can be non-technical. Past retreats had poetry writing, a deep reading of an article,
a movie, and marshmallows over a bonfire. People also made detailed falsifiable predictions
about AI capabilities, gave lightning talks, and designed test suites for frontier models.

## Who we are

Epistemic Experiments is an independent community initiative. We organise this retreat
ourselves, without formal ties to any larger institution.

## Questions

### Is this all about AI?

No. We would run this retreat even if AI was a small matter. The world has other problems that
need inner alignment.

### What kind of people can I expect?

Expect a varied group. Last time we had people who work in neuroscience and mental health,
entrepreneurs, ML interpretability researchers, and people who taught us yoga.

### Who are you, and why do you run this?

We are a group of friends who like to act on the challenges we face, and we want our community
to grow. [Vatsal](https://vatsalmehra.com/) runs
[rationality workshops](https://rationalityworkshops.com) and [Portal](http://enterportal.org).
[Aditya](http://adityaarpitha.com) is a well-known AI safety community builder in India.
[Bhishma](https://www.bhishmaraj.org/about) is an enthusiast of AI safety and of navigating the
impact of AI.

Aditya and Bhishma have followed AI safety since 2022. For the past three years they have run
awareness events at
[IIIT Hyderabad](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1DIoOq8sYGDpu_oW16N7BcGTjfPyn6rOPXlOD6t-VvrU/edit?usp=sharing),
[IIT Madras](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QWX-Rk066cA) and
[EAGx](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rglc4gOcAJc&list=PLL3piKwLOMhpiFuOxO9Qqnoj0SU0gIJQy&index=2). We keep asking whether there are more
people like us. This retreat is our attempt to find you.

### Must I run a session?

Yes. Everyone comes with something to give. A session can be a talk, a game, a puzzle or an open
question, and we accept sessions that are half finished.

### Can I come for part of the retreat?

No. You can arrive earlier or stay longer.

minutes, which depends on how much you like to write. Fill it by 10 October.
